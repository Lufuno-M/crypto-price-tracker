# MarketBrain Restoration Ranking
## 1. Purpose

Ranking determines which unresolved context MarketBrain should restore first.

Ranking does not determine truth, confirmation, invalidation, or resolution.

Ranking answers:

> Given everything currently unresolved, what context deserves restoration now?

## 2. Ranking Unit

The fundamental ranking unit is an Obligation.

Assets provide grouping and navigation context.

An Asset is not itself the thing being ranked.

## 3. Direct Confrontation

Direct confrontation has the strongest priority effect.

Examples include:

- current price entering an Obligation's defined condition zone
- price entering a relevant macro Imbalance / Fair Value Gap
- a liquidity sweep directly relevant to the Obligation
- scheduled evidence approaching the Obligation's timeframe
- new evidence directly addressing the Obligation

These are contextual triggers.

They can elevate an Obligation for restoration.

They do not resolve it.

## 4. Pursuit

Pursuit represents the user's declared current focus.

Pursuit contributes to restoration priority.

Pursuit does not change the truth or importance of an Obligation.

A lower-pursuit Obligation may outrank a higher-pursuit Obligation when it is being directly confronted.
## 5. Asserted Importance

Importance is asserted when an Obligation is created.

It is not inferred from attention, views, clicks, trading frequency, emotional intensity, or time spent.

Importance remains distinct from restoration priority.

An Obligation can be highly important without being the context that needs restoration right now.

## 6. Unresolved Context

Active and neglected Obligations are candidates for restoration.

Confirmed and invalidated Obligations remain part of history but normally do not compete for primary restoration.

Neglect does not mean an Obligation is unimportant.

Neglect may increase restoration priority when unresolved context has been left unattended.

## 7. Neglect

Neglected is a reversible state.

An Obligation may move:

active -> neglected -> active

Neglect is evidence of unresolved context, not evidence of resolution.

Neglect does not automatically increase the truth, importance, or urgency of an Obligation.
## 8. Price Gravity

Price Gravity describes the relationship between current price and relevant macro Imbalances / Fair Value Gaps, liquidity pools, and recent swing highs or lows.

Price Gravity matters to ranking when it is relevant to an unresolved Obligation.

Proximity to a testable market area can increase restoration relevance.

Price Gravity does not resolve an Obligation.

It provides context for determining whether an Obligation is being approached or confronted.

## 9. Evidence Relevance

Relevant evidence has greater restoration value than irrelevant recency.

An observation should influence ranking when it materially relates to an unresolved Obligation.

Recent information alone does not make an Obligation more important.

Evidence can elevate an Obligation when it directly addresses its condition, mechanism, or timeframe.
## 10. Timeframe Relevance

Timeframe relevance determines how close an Obligation is to meaningful confrontation.

Relevance increases when:

- its defined timeframe is approaching
- its condition is approaching
- its condition is currently being confronted
- new evidence falls within its relevant timeframe

Timeframe proximity can increase restoration priority.

Timeframe relevance does not itself resolve an Obligation.

## 11. Restoration Priority

Restoration Priority is the combined judgment of which unresolved Obligation most deserves attention in the current context.

Conceptually:

Restoration Priority =

direct confrontation
+ price gravity relevance
+ pursuit weight
+ asserted importance
+ timeframe relevance
+ meaningful neglect
+ relevant evidence

These inputs are not interchangeable.

They influence priority while preserving the distinction between truth, resolution, and restoration.
## 12. Contextual Hijack

A lower-ranked Obligation may become the immediate restoration priority when current market behavior or evidence directly confronts it.

Example:

Obligation A has high importance and is the primary pursuit, but its condition is not currently being approached.

Obligation B has lower importance, but current price enters its defined Fair Value Gap or directly relevant condition zone.

Obligation B may temporarily become the restoration priority.

This is a ranking change, not a model-state change.

The Obligation does not become more true because it was elevated.

It becomes more relevant because the current context is confronting it.

## 13. Hijack Is Temporary

A contextual hijack is temporary.

When the confrontation passes without resolution, ordinary restoration ranking may resume.

A hijack does not rewrite:

- asserted importance
- pursuit
- status
- history
- resolution

## 14. Asset Pursuit

Pursuit may concentrate restoration on one primary asset at a time.

This does not erase unresolved Obligations in other assets.

Other assets remain part of the unresolved context and may become relevant when directly confronted.
## 15. Ranking Cannot Rewrite Truth

Ranking is subordinate to the ontology and resolution model.

An Obligation remains what it is regardless of where it appears in the ranking.

Ranking cannot:

- confirm an Obligation
- invalidate an Obligation
- resolve an Obligation
- change asserted importance
- change the user's historical record

Ranking only determines which unresolved context should be restored first.

## 16. Restoration Principle

The highest-ranked Obligation is not necessarily the most important, the most recent, or the most viewed.

It is the unresolved context whose restoration is most justified by the current relationship between the user's commitments and market behavior.

The system hierarchy is:

Ontology defines what exists.

Resolution defines what happened.

Ranking defines what should be restored first.

Interface expresses the result.

MarketBrain exists to restore the right context at the right time.
